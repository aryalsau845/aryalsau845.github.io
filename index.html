About

I am a PhD candidate in Civil Engineering at Michigan State University, specializing in hydrologic and hydrodynamic modeling, flood-risk assessment, and water-resources analysis. My work investigates how river and floodplain systems respond to changing climate and water management.

As a Graduate Research Assistant in the Multi-scale FEW Lab, I study hydrologic dynamics across Asian river basins, analyze multi-model climate projections, and work with NASA SWOT satellite observations to improve understanding of river and reservoir states.

Before beginning my PhD, I earned a Bachelor of Engineering in Civil Engineering from Tribhuvan University, Institute of Engineering, Pulchowk Campus in Nepal. I also worked in civil engineering, river basin research, and infrastructure assessment. I am an Engineer-in-Training (E.I.T.) with interests in flooding, drainage, and resilience.

Research

My research integrates physics-based simulation, satellite data, and climate-model ensembles to understand hydrologic change and improve flood and water-resources analysis.

Project 1 · Asian Water Tower
Hydrologic Change & Flood Extremes

Developed multi-decadal assessments of river basin hydrology across Asia, examining spatial differences in water availability, flooding, and hydrologic extremes over 1979–2018.

Project 2 · Climate Projections
Future Flood & Drought Risks

Led multi-model ensemble analyses to investigate regional changes in discharge and concurrent flood–drought extremes under future climate scenarios.

Project 3 · Satellite Hydrology
NASA SWOT & River Modeling

Collaborated on validating NASA Surface Water and Ocean Topography (SWOT) observations for the Ganges River, with broader work on constraining river and reservoir states in hydrodynamic models.

Project 4 · Water Infrastructure
Climate–Hydropower Interactions

Reviewed climate and hydropower dynamics in Asian river basins, focusing on infrastructure risk, changing water availability, and policy challenges.
